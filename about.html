One salon.
Everybody.

In Twi, adepa means a good thing. We built the salon we wanted for our own families: one place where mum, dad and the children can all sit down on the same Saturday.

Why unisex,
and why it matters.

Most families in Accra visit three different places for hair: a barber for him, a braider for her, and somewhere patient enough for the little ones. That is three trips, three waits and most of a weekend.

Adepa House puts barbers, braiders, a loctician and a nail desk in the same room. You book once, arrive together and leave together.

Children get
their own corner.

Small chairs, a screen with cartoons, and barbers who are in no hurry. A first haircut takes as long as it takes, and you go home with the photo and the first curl.

For girls’ braids we keep extensions light and partings loose, so nothing pulls at the hairline.
